# Product Principles

> Last Updated: 2026-07-08

## Purpose

This document defines the product philosophy of **Nachim**.

It describes **why Nachim exists**, **what value it should provide**, and **how product decisions should be made**.

Every new feature should be evaluated against these principles.

---

# Product Vision

Nachim is a platform that helps university students achieve their goals by automatically organizing scattered information and managing their progress.

Our mission is not to tell users **what to choose**, but to help them **follow through on the choices they make**.

---

# Product Mission

Help students spend less time searching for information and more time taking action.

Students should focus on achieving their goals—not managing schedules, deadlines, and scattered notices.

---

# Core Philosophy

## 1. Users make decisions.

AI does not make decisions.

AI provides information.

AI suggests possibilities.

AI summarizes data.

The final decision always belongs to the user.

---

## 2. Information should become action.

Collecting information alone has little value.

Every important piece of information should help users take action.

Examples:

* Save
* Schedule
* Reminder
* Progress

Nachim exists to bridge the gap between **information** and **execution**.

---

## 3. Automation is more valuable than complexity.

Users should not manually manage information whenever automation is possible.

Nachim automatically:

* collects information
* filters information
* creates schedules
* manages deadlines

The best feature is the one users never have to think about.

---

## 4. Data is our greatest asset.

Technology changes.

AI changes.

Frameworks change.

Student data and user behavior become stronger over time.

Every product decision should strengthen the quality of our data.

---

## 5. Save users' time.

The most valuable resource for students is time.

Every feature should reduce:

* searching
* repetitive work
* forgotten deadlines
* unnecessary clicks

---

## 6. Simplicity over features.

A small number of well-designed features is better than many unfinished features.

Do not build features simply because they are possible.

Build features because they solve real problems.

---

# Product Values

Every feature should satisfy at least one of the following values.

## Information

Provide the right information.

---

## Organization

Help users organize their goals.

---

## Automation

Reduce manual work.

---

## Execution

Help users complete what they planned.

---

## Insight

Provide meaningful data that users cannot easily obtain elsewhere.

---

# What Nachim Is Not

Nachim is **not**:

* an AI that chooses a career for users
* a chatbot that replaces thinking
* a simple calendar application
* a notice board
* another job search website

---

# What Nachim Is

Nachim is:

* an information hub
* an execution platform
* a career management system
* a data-driven platform
* a long-term companion for university students

---

# Product Priorities

When making product decisions, prioritize:

1. User value
2. Simplicity
3. Automation
4. Data quality
5. Scalability

Technology should support these priorities, never replace them.

---

# MVP Principles

The MVP exists to validate user value.

The MVP should:

* solve one important problem well
* launch quickly
* collect meaningful user feedback
* collect high-quality data

Do not delay launch by adding unnecessary features.

---

---

# Product Decision Checklist

Before adding any feature, ask:

* Does this solve a real student problem?
* Does it save users time?
* Does it encourage action?
* Does it improve data quality?
* Can the MVP succeed without it?

If the answer is **No**, reconsider the feature.
